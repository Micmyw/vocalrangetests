THE PROCESS

How this vocal range test works

A short room check and two comfortable notes are enough to measure the distance between your lowest and highest captured pitches.

  1. 01

    Check your room

    Allow microphone access, then stay quiet for three seconds while the tool measures background noise.

  2. 02

    Sing your lowest note

    Settle on the lowest comfortable note you can hold without forcing or dropping into vocal fry.

  3. 03

    Sing your highest note

    Use your highest comfortable stable note. A natural head voice or falsetto is okay.

  4. 04

    Read your range

    See both notes, their distance in semitones, the exact octave span, and conventional range overlap.

02

BETTER INPUT, CLEARER RESULT

How to get a more accurate result

  • Use a quiet room.Background sound makes a stable pitch harder to separate.
  • Prefer the built-in microphone.Avoid Bluetooth processing or music playing from the same device.
  • Sing “ah” or “oo.”A clear vowel usually produces steadier pitch evidence than speech.
  • Stay comfortable.Do not squeeze an extreme high note or manufacture a low note with vocal fry.
  • Settle before holding.A gentle slide is fine, but the final pitch needs to stop drifting.
03

READING THE MEASUREMENT

Understanding your vocal range result

Lowest and highest note

These are the two stable endpoints captured in this test—not every pitch you might reach on a different day.

Semitone span

The musical distance between the two displayed note names. Twelve semitones equal one octave.

Decimal octave span

The exact frequency ratio between the two measurements, shown to two decimal places.

Range overlap

A comparison with conventional reference ranges. It is context, not a definitive voice classification.

04

A USEFUL SNAPSHOT, NOT A LABEL

What this test can—and can’t—tell you

The test can estimate the measurable distance between one comfortable low note and one comfortable high note. It filters for a clear signal, stable pitch, steady ending, and possible octave ambiguity before accepting an endpoint.

It cannot determine tessitura, vocal weight, passaggi, training level, fach, or professional voice type. Technique, health, warm-up, microphone processing, and whether you use head voice or falsetto can all change the captured endpoints.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Common questions

How long should I hold each note?

Aim for about three seconds. The test can lock sooner once it has enough stable evidence, so you do not have to hit an exact timer.

Can I use head voice or falsetto?

Yes for your highest comfortable note. Because of that, the range-overlap result is deliberately presented as an estimate rather than a definitive voice type.

Why did the test ask me to try again?

The signal may have been too quiet, clipped, noisy, still sliding, or unstable near the end. The retry message tells you what to adjust.

Does this measure my tessitura?

No. Measured range is the distance between two captured endpoints. Tessitura describes where your voice is most comfortable and usable across music.
