A guided two-note check

How this vocal range test works

A short room check and two comfortable notes are enough. Live pitch, input level, and stability feedback show what the test is hearing.

  1. 01

    Check your room

    Allow microphone access, then stay quiet for three seconds while the tool learns the background level.

  2. 02

    Settle on your lowest note

    Slide down gently and hold the lowest comfortable note that remains clear—not vocal fry.

  3. 03

    Settle on your highest note

    Slide up gently and hold a comfortable high note. Head voice or falsetto is okay.

  4. 04

    Read your range

    See both notes, their frequencies, the semitone span, the octave span, and cautious reference-range overlap.

Before you sing

A clearer result starts with a comfortable voice

Warm up first, use an easy vowel such as “ah” or “oo,” and stop if anything feels strained.

  • Choose a quiet roomBackground sound makes a stable pitch harder to separate.
  • Use the built-in microphoneAvoid Bluetooth processing or music playing from the same device.
  • Let the pitch settleA gentle slide is fine; hold once the final note stops moving.
  • Measure comfort, not forceDo not squeeze an extreme high note or manufacture a low note with vocal fry.

Understanding your vocal range result

A vocal range is a distance between notes. It is useful context, but it is not your entire vocal identity.

01

Lowest and highest note

The two stable endpoints captured in this session—not every pitch you might reach on a different day.

02

Semitone span

The musical distance between the two note names. Twelve semitones equal one octave.

03

Octave span

The exact frequency ratio between the measurements, displayed as a decimal number of octaves.

04

Range overlap

A comparison with conventional reference ranges. Treat it as context, not a diagnosis or definitive voice classification.

A useful snapshot, not a label

The test does not determine tessitura, vocal weight, passaggi, training level, fach, or professional voice type. Warm-up, technique, health, microphone processing, and register choice can all move the endpoints.

Local audio processing

Microphone privacy, without the fine print

Your microphone signal is analyzed in this browser. The site does not record, save, upload, or export the audio used for your test.

Production analytics can record page and test-lifecycle events, but not your notes, frequencies, range, audio, or microphone settings.

Good to know

Common questions

How long should I hold each note?

Hold a comfortable steady pitch while the stability bar fills. The test accepts the note once it has enough stable evidence and a steady ending.

Can I use head voice or falsetto?

Yes for your highest comfortable note. Because of that, the range-overlap result is deliberately presented as an estimate rather than a definitive voice type.

Why did the test ask me to try again?

The signal may have been too quiet, clipped, noisy, still sliding, or unstable near the end. The feedback tells you what to adjust before you listen again.

Does this measure my tessitura?

No. Measured range is the distance between two captured endpoints. Tessitura describes where your voice is most comfortable and usable across music.

When does the microphone turn off?

It turns off when your result is ready, when you choose Stop test, when the page is hidden, or when you leave or refresh the page.
